# Level authoring

Status: approved v1. Format spec in `docs/architecture.md` → "Level".

## Format reminders
- One char = one 16×16 tile or an entity spawn. Legend in `[legend]`, grid in `[map].grid`.
- All rows must be the same width. Row 0 is the top. Usually 14 rows tall.
- Exactly one `P` (player start), at least one goal (`G`), one checkpoint per stage (`C`).
- Format v2 (D-022, full list in `docs/architecture.md` → "Level"): `"?" = { tile = "oblaten_box", contains = "mozartkugel" }` (contains: `oblate`, `mozartkugel`, `zaunerstollen`, `oblaten_10`; only on `brick`/`oblaten_box`), `"o" = { entity = "oblate" }`, `"C" = { entity = "checkpoint" }`, `"s" = { entity = "sign", text = "stage.salzburg.sign_bus", style = "bus" }` (style `street`/`bus`/`ortsende`), enemies by catalog name. `[meta]` needs `time_limit`; `background = [{ sprite = "bg_rooftops", factor = 8, y = 0 }]` (factor in 1/16 camera speed; optional `y` = px above the screen bottom); optional `min_jump_window`; optional `camera_top = <level px>` (side levels only, D-103: the camera never above it; set it when a jump the stage invites from ground more than ~5 rows above a street with hazards would lift them off screen: street row top + 16 − 180).
- Festungsbahn: `"F" = { entity = "festungsbahn", to = [8, -5] }` — `to` is its other end point in tiles relative to its cell (required); the car (3 tiles wide, 24 px high) starts left-aligned in that cell with its bottom on the cell's bottom edge. It **waits for its rider** (D-030): at its lower end (the end with the larger y) it waits until the player stands on it, travels up 150 frames, waits 60 frames at the top, travels back down and waits for the next rider. A car placed at its upper end (`to` goes down) first travels down. A fallen player can only call the car back while its lower end is on screen (the camera never scrolls back), so the stairs under the track are the real way out. Only its top is solid (from above), so the player boards it from above (keep its top a few px below the boarding ledge) and walks through its sides. `levels lint` requires 3 tiles of free space above the car along the whole track, and stair-stepped rock under it: from the floor under the track to the floor beyond its upper end, no step up higher than 3 tiles (D-027/D-028: falling off is never a softlock). Keep the stairs below the car's path.
- Schafbergbahn (M4, D-067): `"Z" = { entity = "schafbergbahn", to = [24, -6] }` — the Festungsbahn's waiting train with its own numbers: body 64×32 (4 × 2 tiles, left-aligned in its cell, bottom on the cell bottom), `to` required with 4 <= |dx| <= 32, travel = max(|dx|, |dy|) × 24 frames, 90 frames at the top. With whole-tile `to` both roofs lie on the tile grid: put the station platforms' tops exactly at the roof (spawn cell row − 1 at the lower end) so she walks on and off. Its height follows the drawn rail pixel for pixel (1 px per 4 px on a 1:4 track). `levels lint` checks the **swept body**: no solid or one-way tile may overlap the body anywhere along the track, nor the 3 tiles above its roof. The body's lowest point over a column is where its **lower right corner** enters it (on a track rising right): for a train spawned with its body left edge at column c0 and bottom y B, a tile in column c ≥ c0 + 4 needs its top ≥ B − 4 (c − c0 − 4) px on a 1:4 track (Stage 5: the body in columns 52–55 with B = 240 needs tops ≥ 240 − 4 (c − 56) px, e.g. row 14 from column 60 on). The pit recovery check applies too (bottomless gorges are skipped). Jumps don't inherit its speed: a standing hop on the rising roof lands 15–33 px further back (tap / full hop), riding down 17–36 px forward; put hop rewards over the uphill half of the roof (test level `00_schafbergbahn`). A running jump off the moving roof clears wide gaps, so a gorge the ride must cross needs to be wider than a running jump from anywhere on it reaches.
- Fensterläden (M6, D-094): `"f" = { entity = "fensterladen", period = 240, open = 150, phase = 0, tile = "wall_village_bg" }` — a guest-house shutter: a stationary one-way 32×8 body, left-aligned in its cell with its **top on the cell's top edge** (2 tiles wide). It runs on the **stage clock** (frames of play since the stage start or the last respawn): f = (clock + `phase`) mod `period`; open (landing, standing, jump-through) for f < `open`, closed (no collision) for the rest. `period` 120–480 (default 240), `open` 60..=period − 30 (default 150), `phase` 0..period − 1 (default 0). Two shutters with the same `period` keep their relative phase exactly, whatever the player does: with `open` 150, phases 0 and 60 are open together for 90 frames per cycle. A closing shutter drops its rider (no damage), so on the required route put solid ground under it; ≤ 3 tiles above what it is reached from; 3 tiles of air above it (`levels lint`, as for the other platforms). Check that no running jump from a nearby ledge skips a shutter staircase (a running jump covers ~9–10 tiles; the grey box needed 10 columns of street between its balcony and the annex). Test level `00_m6_shutters` (replays `m6_shutters_*`).
- Boats (M5, D-077): `"T" = { entity = "traundl", to = [6, 0], phase = N }` — the Gondel's free-running ping-pong with a 48×16 hull (left-aligned in its cell on the deck row 11, bottom on the water line), `to = [dx, 0]` with 2 <= |dx| <= 8 (dy must be 0), 30 frames at each end, 1 px/frame (period 2 × (30 + 16 |dx|): 252 for `[6, 0]`), never waits for a rider; mark both track ends with a `dalben` prop (front layer, in the air cell above the surface). `"D" = { entity = "dampfer", to = [40, 0] }` — the paddle steamer: 160×16 hull (10 tiles), `to = [dx, 0]` with 8 <= dx <= 64 (rightwards only), waits for its rider like the Festungsbahn, travels dx × 20 frames (0.8 px/frame) and then stays **moored** at the far end until the level resets; put its deck flush with the quay at the spawn and with a pier at the far end. While it travels its paddle wheel (32×20 at the hull's middle, from 4 px above the water line to 16 px below) hurts the player: a rider never touches it, a swimmer (or anyone standing under the hull) does. Both: the 3 tiles above the deck stay free along the track (`levels lint`); the pit-recovery check skips columns over **swim** water and passes when the exit is swim water (a fall is a swim; lethal water is looked through, D-084). Tiles: `steg` (one-way pier planks), `kai` (quay wall), `seegrund` (lake bed), `poller` (bollard, 1 high) are solid except `steg`. Test level `00_m5_boats` (replays `m5_boats_*`).
- Props (D-030/D-031): `"S" = { entity = "prop", sprite = "sphaera", dx = 8 }` draws a catalog prop sprite (`sphaera`, `pegasus`, `fountain_water`, `zwerg`, `residenzbrunnen`, `geburtshaus`; `levels lint` checks the name, list `jnr_core::PROP_SPRITES`) with its bottom centre on the cell's bottom centre, moved `dx` px (optional, ±64; 8 = centred between two columns). No collision: put solid tiles under/behind it. Default layer front (after the tiles, behind the player); `layer = "back"` draws it before the tiles (façades behind the street). Tile `invisible` is solid but never drawn (under a prop like the Sphaera); `facade` and `arch_under` are solid autotiles, and `arch`/`arch_under` autotile as one family (an arch on its underside shows no edge between them).
- Entity on a tile: an entity's cell is air unless its entry adds `tile = "..."`, e.g. `"w" = { entity = "nordic_walker", tile = "wall_village_bg" }` or `"o" = { entity = "oblate", tile = "wall_village_bg" }` (walkers/Oblaten in front of a house wall without a hole in it). Only non-colliding tiles (`wall_village_bg`, `railing_bg`, `roof_bg`); solid/one-way/hazard tiles under an entity are a parse error. Needs one legend char per entity+tile combination.
- Sitting Taube on a perch: `{ entity = "taube_sitting", dy = -8 }` — `dy` (−15..=15 px, default 0, negative = up) moves its feet from the cell bottom, so it can sit on a prop or ledge between two cell bottoms (St. Wolfgang's mitre is 40 px above the ground: put it in the cell whose bottom is 48 px up and use `dy = 8`, or 32 px up and `dy = -8`). It rises and flies from there as usual. Use a positive `dy` only where the cell below is air, or the Taube sinks into the tile.
- Bienenschwarm: `{ entity = "bienenschwarm", dir = 0..=7, len = 2..=4, phase = N, dy = -8 }` — `dy` (±8 px, default 0, negative = up) moves its anchor from the cell centre; `dy = -8` lifts a swarm anchored just above the ground so its return arcs dip less into it (Brunnwinkl #2–#5); a diagonal run's return can still touch the ground by ~1 px at its low point, see the „E12 art-review notes" in `docs/backlog.md`.
- M3 enemies (D-058, D-061): `{ entity = "pilger" }` walks towards the player at activation and **walks off ledges** (place groups of 2–3 one cell apart; `lead = true` on the first gives the pennant sprite, same behaviour). `{ entity = "gams" }` stands 60 / crouches 16 / hops 2 tiles (apex ~2 tiles) and never leaves its ledge: it only hops where the floor under the whole 2-tile hop is continuous and no wall is in its body rows, else it turns or hops in place. `{ entity = "steinschlag", period = 150, phase = 0, range = 6 }` is a rock niche (all fields optional; `period` 90–300 frames, `phase` 0 or 41..period, `range` 4–10 tiles): place it in an empty cell under solid rock; its counter runs only while Resi is within `range` tiles and the niche is fully on screen below the HUD, then 40 frames of wobble and pebbles, then the rock falls and shatters on the first solid or one-way tile below (a one-way net 3 tiles above a path is a shelter). Keep the niche bottom 4–6 tiles above the ground it hits. Test level: `content/levels/00_m3_enemies.level`.
- M4 enemies (D-068, D-069): `{ entity = "operettensaenger" }` (no fields) stands and faces Resi; while she is within 10 tiles his 180-frame cycle runs (pauses outside): inhale 0–39, the high C 40–87, idle 88–179 (the 92-frame window). The C is a 12 px column from his head straight up, 8 px taller per frame to 80 px (5 tiles); it passes one-way tiles and ends under the first solid tile above him. It hurts on any contact, also when she falls onto him: stomp him only outside it. On a one-way balcony 4 tiles above the street he is harmless to the street (the safe introduction); keep anything that invites a jump from below out of his column, and give a street singer ≥ 6 free tiles either side. `{ entity = "souvenirstand", period = 150, phase = 0, range = 8 }` (all optional; `period` 90–300, `phase` 0 or 31..period, `range` 4–10 tiles) goes in the air cell in front of (left of) its counter: build the stand from tiles, `standl` (solid, 1 high) to the right of the spawner and `standl_dach` (one-way) 2 rows above the counter (1 air row between: the vendor stands there); grey-box levels use `solid`/`oneway`. Its counter runs only while Resi is left of it, within `range` and the spawner cell is on screen: a 30-frame telegraph, then a 10×10 snow globe rolls left at 1 px/frame (gravity, off ledges, lands on one-way tops) until it shatters on a solid wall (put a 1-high curb in front of the first stand), drops into water, or leaves the screen on the left; at most 2 of a stand's globes are alive. Globes are stompable (100, in the chain) and hurt from the side. Test level: `content/levels/00_m4_enemies.level`.
- Swim water (M5, D-076): tiles `see_wasser` (surface row), `see_wasser_tief` (below it) and `steg_pfahl` (water with a pier post, under deck ends) are `TileKind::Swim`: never lethal, Resi swims while her chest (8 px below her hitbox top) is in it. She floats at the surface, DOWN dives (sinks up to 1 px/frame), JUMP below the surface strokes (~24 px up), JUMP at the surface jumps out like a walking jump (held: Normal feet ~53 px above the water, big ~45 px, so decks/quays 1 tile above the water are reachable in every form; a tap is not enough), swims 1.25 px/frame. The lethal `*_water` tiles are unchanged. `levels lint`: every swim column must end on a **solid** lake bed inside the level. Oblaten and lake enemies may sit in swim cells: `{ entity = "oblate", tile = "see_wasser_tief" }`. Items, axes and walking enemies are lost in swim water. Test level: `content/levels/00_m5_swim.level`. In an auto-scroll zone a left-facing solid face over a swim surface may rise at most **2 tiles** above it (big Resi's held jump-out reaches ~45 px; over decks and ground 3, D-084, `levels lint`). Every M5 kind together over swim water in a zone: `content/levels/00_m5_lake.level`. A solver window whose route swims before it stands again is measured to its next frame on the ground (D-084).
- Reisebus (M4, D-070): `{ entity = "reisebus" }` or `{ entity = "reisebus", stop = 4 }` (`stop` 1–16 tiles, optional). Place it in the **leftmost of its 4 cells** on the street row (feet on the cell bottom; the 64×32 bus covers that cell and the 3 to its right, hitbox 60×28: rows `row-1` and `row`). It is parked until Resi's centre is within 160 px (10 tiles) of its centre, telegraphs 60 frames, then drives left at 1.5 px/frame **through everything** (tiles, enemies) until its right edge leaves the screen; with `stop = N` it halts exactly N tiles left of where it was parked for a 120-frame photo stop, 30 more frames of hazard lights, then drives on. Its roof always bounces (no damage, no points, in every state); any other contact hurts, also parked or stopped; the Hackl bounces off. `levels lint`: its lane (its two hitbox rows, from its own cells to 24 tiles left, or the level's left edge) must have no solid or one-way tile, so it is never seen driving through a wall: put dodge spots (a terrace, a shelter roof) above the lane: a top 3 tiles above the street clears its 28 px roof by 20 px. Every bus needs a dodge in view. Test level: `content/levels/00_m4_bus.level`.
- M5 lake enemies (D-078 … D-080; rows: decks on 11, the water line at the top of 12): `{ entity = "schwan", tile = "see_wasser" }` (no other fields; any entity may sit on a swim tile, none on lethal `*_water`, E30) floats in a **surface-row swim-water cell**, its 16×12 hitbox 4 px under the water line (8 px below a deck top: a deck rider is out of its reach). Rested at home, fully on screen and with Resi within 96 px it hisses 32 frames, charges 96 px (1.5 px/frame) towards her side **through every tile**, paddles home at 0.5 and rests 60 (a 348-frame cycle): keep its charge path free of solids, piers and reeds. Stomp 200 („Beleidigt!"), axe Defeat. `{ entity = "gelse" }` (no fields; 8×8 hitbox centred in its cell) hovers until it has been **fully on screen for 60 frames**, then homes in on her head for 480 frames (0.75 px/frame per axis, it overshoots) and leaves upwards; it flies through every tile but never into water, lethal or swim (it waits at the water line above a diver). Stomp 100, axe Defeat. Keep it above reeds; never on a screen with a regatta boat. `{ entity = "regattaboot", tile = "see_wasser" }` (no other fields) goes in the **left of its 2 cells in the surface row**: hitbox 24×56 from the masthead (28 px above the decks) to the keel (12 px under the water line). It is parked until Resi is within 160 px of its centre **and** it is fully on screen (mind the vertical framing: a perch high above the lane can push the keel off the screen bottom, then it never starts), telegraphs 60 frames and races left at 1.5 px/frame through everything, never stopping. Masthead from above (only the 8 px around the mast, the top 20 px): a bounce without damage or points; falling onto the sail beside it hurts; any other contact hurts; a diver 13 px down passes under it; axe Bounce. `levels lint`: its lane (its hitbox rows, 24 tiles to the left) has no solid or one-way tile, like the bus. Test level: `content/levels/00_m5_enemies.level`.
- M6 Kurgast (D-095): `{ entity = "kurgast" }` (no fields) walks towards Resi at 0.375 px/frame and turns at walls, enemies on foot and ledges (the Nordic-Walker). Stomped (100 the first time only) he lies down where he stood in a 16×16 deck chair, a one-way top she can stand and jump from (1 tile up: a short jump from it reaches a ledge 4 tiles above the floor), harmless; walkers turn at it, chargers and notes pass. He rests 300 frames and wakes 40, both paused while she stands on him, then stands up (12×22, feet kept) and walks on; standing beside the chair when he gets up hurts. Put a chair spot on flat ground with him penned by walls ≥ 2 high, and never make a required passage need the chair. Test level: `content/levels/00_m6_kurgast.level`.
- Enemies patrol on frame counters from activation (one screen + 320 px ahead of the camera), so where they are when the player arrives depends on the player's speed; Selfie-Tourists walk off ledges into pits and turn at walls and other enemies; the Fiaker ignores tiles on its row. `{ entity = "selfie_tourist", still = true }` (never walks) is a fixture for `00_*` test levels only; `levels lint` rejects it in stages.
- Grey-box test levels: `content/levels/00_items.level` (items/power states), `content/levels/00_enemies.level` (enemies, Festungsbahn), `content/levels/00_climb_test.level` (climb camera, cleft, 6-tile drop, a 9-column `steig` shaft).

## Climb levels (Stage 4 on, D-053 … D-055; rules in `game-design.md` → "Climbing rules")
- `[meta] camera = "climb"` (default `"side"`: the dead-zone camera of Stages 1–3). Grounded, the camera settles with Resi's feet at screen y 148 (2 px/frame); in the air it holds still unless her top rises above screen y 40 (follows up) or her feet sink below 148 (follows down, but at most 80 px below the last ground's framing). **Death line**: her hitbox top below the screen bottom kills her (a fall out of the level). So a drop of ≤ 6 tiles lands on screen, 7–9 tiles land off screen (forbidden, `levels lint` rejects them: fall depth), ≥ 10 tiles or bottomless is a cleft that kills ~40 frames after the edge. Start and respawn frame the feet at 148 at once; the goal sequence settles the feet at 164 (pole base framed like Stages 1–3). Numbers: `[camera] climb_*` in `content/tuning.toml`.
- Level entities in climb levels activate only when their spawn cell is within 64 px of the screen vertically (as well as within one screen to the right), so enemies high up the mountain don't wake off screen.
- Tiles: `kalkfels` (solid limestone, the mountain mass), `kalkfels_bg` (decorative back wall, no collision: shaft interior, caves), `steig` (one-way wooden plank; `steig_wall` is the same plank drawn over the `kalkfels_bg` back wall, like `balcony` over `wall_village_bg`: use it inside shafts) and `netz` (one-way rockfall net). Shaft planks: 5 wide, 3 tiles apart, each overlapping the next by one column; a shaft is ≤ 9 columns wide inside its walls (`levels lint` warns above 9: a run of non-solid cells between two vertical solid walls, ≥ 6 rows tall, with a one-way tile in it).
- Background layers take `factor_y` (0..=32, default 0): the layer sinks `(max camera y − camera y) × factor_y / 16` px as the camera climbs; it sits exactly as without `factor_y` at the level bottom.
- `levels lint` (every level): a `steinschlag` niche's bottom is 4–6 tiles above the first **solid** tile below it (one-way tiles in between, e.g. a `netz` shelter or a `steig` plank, are skipped, D-062); a `durchkriechstein`'s `len` tunnel cells are empty with solid above and below, its mouth (left) and exit (right) have 2 empty rows over a floor at the tunnel floor's height.
- The solver ranks climb levels by x + height gained (in a shaft "furthest right" sees no progress), and a shifted jump's recovery must also end with the feet at most 8 px lower than the original route's (so landing on the plank below counts as recovered only if she climbs back up in time; the climb re-plan margin is 60 frames instead of 30).

## Jump reach (check `content/tuning.toml`; update this table when tuning changes)
| Situation | Max height | Max gap |
|---|---|---|
| walking jump | 4 tiles (65 px) | ~5 tiles (82 px airborne distance) |
| running jump | 5 tiles (81 px) | ~9 tiles (157 px airborne distance) |

Measured in M0 by `crates/jnr-core/tests/feel.rs`. Gaps "max" means flat ground to flat ground; leave a safety margin of 1 tile in Stage 1.

## Pacing per section
1. **Introduce** a mechanic where failing is harmless (no pit under it).
2. **Develop** it with mild danger.
3. **Twist**: combine it with an earlier mechanic.
4. **Breather**: a few safe screens with Oblaten as a reward.
Place Oblaten to show the intended path (arcs show jumps). Hidden blocks reward exploration, never block progress.

## Commands
- `cargo xtask levels lint <id>` – structure, legend, references, text keys
- `cargo xtask solve <id>` – proves the stage can be completed (beam search over the real simulation, ~0.6 s for the testbed); prints a summary and writes the difficulty report `$CARGO_TARGET_DIR/reports/solver/<id>.{md,json}` (frames, search deaths, every jump with its frame, x, hold and **jump window**, the narrowest one) plus `preview/solver/<id>_narrowest.png`. If it fails: furthest grounded x and frame, and `preview/solver/<id>_stuck.png` there.
- `cargo xtask solve <id> --write` – also writes `tests/replays/<id>_solver.replay`; commit it (the `solver` verify step fails without it). `cargo xtask solve --all` for every level (that is the `solver` verify step)
- Jump window = how many frames the JUMP press can be shifted (within ±12) while the player still survives the jump and can still get as far as the original 2 s after landing (16 px tolerance; D-033): either with the route's later inputs, or by re-planning from the landing with a small beam search (so a later press that would have to move by a frame doesn't count against this jump). The last jump's checkpoint is the finish, so slack there is measured against the route's finish frame + 30. The `bounds` column says why each side ends: `fail` = the level's real limit, `route` = only the solver's route prevents a bigger shift (window shown as `≥ N`, ignored for the narrowest), `max` = ±12. Read it from the report table; look at the `_narrowest.png` shot.
- `cargo xtask scene <id> --x <px>` – screenshot at a position
- `cargo xtask filmstrip <replay>` – view the solver's run

## Difficulty guidelines (overall: medium, see `game-design.md`)
- Narrowest jump window on the main route (solver report): ≥ 6 frames in stages 1–3, ≥ 4 frames later. Bonus routes may be harder.
- No blind jumps: the landing spot must be visible on screen before the jump.
- Never place an enemy directly at a landing spot in the first two stages.
