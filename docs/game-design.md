# Game design

Status: **approved v1**. Title: **„Oblatenjagd"**.

## Premise

The hero, **Resi** – a Salzburg local in a Dirndl – wants one thing: a fresh slice of Zaunerstollen at **Café Zauner in Bad Ischl**. But Postbus 150 (Salzburg → Bad Ischl, a real line) is hopelessly full of tourists, so the hero sets off on foot along the old route of the *Ischlerbahn* (the Salzkammergut-Lokalbahn, closed 1957). On the way: tour groups, Fiaker, pilgrims, swans, regatta sailors and the entire Salzkammergut cliché machine.

Tone: affectionate satire. We make fun of tourism, kitsch, Austrian bureaucracy and local pride, never of individual people or groups in a mean way. Lederhosen and Dirndl are worn by everybody, including the enemies.

Language: all in-game text is German with Austrian flavour ("Servus", "Grüß Gott", "Jause", "Hackl", "Bim"). See `.claude/skills/german-satire-writing/`.

## Core gameplay (Super Mario Bros. style)

- Side-scrolling from left to right; the camera never scrolls back left (like SMB). Only exception: a moving platform carrying Resi left past the screen edge takes the camera along (D-051). From M5 a level may have an **auto-scroll zone** where the screen moves on its own and pushes Resi along (Stage 6, D-075); D-051 doesn't apply inside it.
- Run, jump with variable height (hold to jump higher), stomp enemies from above.
- Collect **Zauner Oblaten** (the coins). 100 Oblaten = 1 extra life.
- Hit **Oblatenkisten** (the "?" blocks) from below to release Oblaten or power-ups.
- Lives, score, countdown timer, checkpoint halfway through each stage.
- Each stage ends at a **Maibaum** (maypole) instead of a flagpole: the higher you grab it, the more points.

### Difficulty: medium

Precise SMB-style platforming, but without SMB's harsh punishment:
- 3 lives at the start; extra lives from 100 Oblaten and Zaunerstollen.
- One checkpoint per stage; dying respawns there.
- Game over → „Weiter?" restarts the **current stage** with 3 lives (unlimited continues; the score is reset).
- The player's hurtbox is a bit smaller than the sprite, and stomps are generous (landing on an enemy's upper half counts).
- No deaths the player can't see coming: hazards are visible before the jump that leads to them.
- Jump windows (measured by the solver): ≥ 6 frames in stages 1–3, ≥ 4 frames later. Optional bonus routes may be harder.

### Level-design reach rules (stages 1–3)

(Stage 4 adds climbing rules on top of these: see "Stage 4: Falkenstein" → "Climbing rules". Stage 5 tightens them: see "Stage 5: St. Wolfgang" → "Difficulty and reach rules". Stage 6 adds rules for swimming and the auto-scroll zone: see "Stage 6: Wolfgangsee" → "Difficulty and reach rules (stage 6)".)

Derived from the measured movement (below) so required jumps keep their ≥ 6-frame windows. The solver report is the final judge; these rules keep designers from fighting it.

| Jump | Reach (flat, ground to ground) | Required in stages 1–3 | Optional / bonus only |
|---|---|---|---|
| Walking jump, held | ~5 tiles across, ~4 tiles up | gaps ≤ 4 tiles, step-ups ≤ 3 tiles | – |
| Running jump, held | ~9 tiles across, ~5 tiles up | gaps 5–6 tiles only with ≥ 8 tiles of flat run-up | gaps 7–8, step-ups of 4–5 tiles |
| Tap jump | ~1.5 tiles up | 1-tile steps | – |

- No ducking (see "Power states"): every required passage is ≥ 2 tiles high, so Zuckerschock Resi (hitbox 30 px) fits everywhere.
- The camera shows ~11 tiles ahead of the player (`follow_x = 144`), so every required jump's landing spot is on screen at take-off; don't place enemies so that they drop or fly into a landing spot that wasn't visible at take-off.
- No required jump onto a 1-tile-wide platform in stage 1.
- Stages 2–3 ramp gently (D-041): the same reach rules and `min_jump_window = 6`; required landings ≥ 2 tiles wide in stage 2 (a gondola roof is 2 tiles), 1-tile landings in stage 3 only over non-lethal ground; **at most one moving hazard** (Taube, note train, bee swarm, charger) acts on any required jump; no enemy reaches a moving platform's track while it can be ridden (D-032 extended to gondolas) or the jump to the Maibaum; Tauben placed by the meeting rule (D-032), what is drawn as solid is solid (D-038).

### Power states

| State | Trigger | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Normal | start | dies on hit |
| **Zuckerschock** (big) | eat a **Mozartkugel** | taller, breaks bricks, survives one hit |
| **Hackl** (thrower, big) | grab **St. Wolfgang's Hackl** from an `H`-Oblatenkiste (from M3, Falkenstein: at the Hackelwurfkapelle, after the legend of the axe throw; from M4 also in St. Wolfgang, where the legend says it landed, D-071), from Normal or Zuckerschock | big like Zuckerschock (breaks bricks), and a new press of RUN throws a Hackl in an arc (D-056) |
| 1-Up | **Zaunerstollen** (rare) | extra life |

- Physics are the same in every state; only the hitbox differs: normal 12×22 px, Zuckerschock and Hackl **12×30 px** (sprite 16×32). Growing keeps the feet in place.
- **Hierarchy** (from M3, D-056): Normal → (Mozartkugel) Zuckerschock → (Hackl) Hackl; a Hackl also turns Normal straight into Hackl. A hit steps down one state: Hackl → Zuckerschock, Zuckerschock → Normal, Normal dies. An item that doesn't raise the state gives its points only (Mozartkugel as Hackl: 1000).
- **No ducking**: DOWN only matters for one-way drop-through (DOWN + JUMP, D-011), from M3 for entering the Durchkriechstein (DOWN at its mouth starts a scripted crawl, D-057) and, from M5, for diving: held while floating at the surface of swim water, it lets Resi sink (D-076). Levels respect this (≥ 2-tile passages everywhere else).
- **Hurt**: Hackl → Zuckerschock or Zuckerschock → Normal, plus **120 invulnerability frames** (2 s; sprite blinks every 4 frames). Enemies and projectiles can't hurt during them, but pits, water, the climb camera's death line, being squeezed by the auto-scroll (D-075) and time-up still kill.
- **Transformation**: every power change (growing, shrinking, Zuckerschock ↔ Hackl) freezes the game for 30 frames while the sprite alternates between the old and the new state's sprite every 4 frames. The timer doesn't run during the freeze.
- **Death**: a hit as Normal, water, falling out of the level, being squeezed between the auto-scrolling screen edge and a wall (D-075), or time-up. Lives −1; then the stage intro screen (with lives) and a respawn at the start or the checkpoint, always as Normal. Swim water (Stage 6, D-076) never kills.
- **Swimming** (from M5, D-076): in swim water every form swims with the same numbers; no stomps and no Hackl throws while swimming. See "Stage 6: Wolfgangsee" → "Swimming".

These targets are automated game-feel tests (`validation.md`, `crates/jnr-core/src/tests/feel.rs`). The M0 values are close to Super Mario Bros. (walk ~1.5, run ~2.5 px/frame, max fall 4 px/frame, running jump ~5 tiles high and ~9 tiles far), which is what we want; the long running jump is a ceiling for optional routes, not a gap size for required jumps (see "Level-design reach rules"). The stomp bounce gets its own tuning keys in M1; the engine picks values that hit the targets above.

## Stage 1: Salzburg (Milestone 1 scope)

Length **262 columns** (~4190 px, D-031; measured at M1 acceptance: the solver finishes in 1601 frames ≈ 27 s, a relaxed full run takes ~65 s, explorers longer; 81 Oblaten in the stage), 17 rows high (3 empty rows on top for the goal camera, D-030; the street's top is row 15, rows 15–16 are ground or water). `time_limit = 300` (generous on purpose for the first stage). The route follows the real walk from the main station through the old town: Südtiroler Platz (Postbus stops) → Mirabellgarten → Makartsteg → Hanuschplatz (big bus hub) → Getreidegasse → Residenzplatz → Domplatz → Kapitelplatz → Festungsbahn.

**Stomping** (all stompable enemies): a stomp happens when the player is falling (vy > 0) and the player's feet were above the enemy hitbox's vertical middle on the previous frame (generous, "upper half counts"). Several enemies in the same frame are all stomped. Any other contact with an enemy or projectile hurts. Bumping a brick or box from below defeats an enemy standing on it (100 points).

**Stomp chain**: stomps without touching the ground score 100, 200, 400, 800, 1000, 2000, 4000, 8000, then +1 life for each further stomp (Mozartkugel-Verkäufer as the first stomp of a chain starts at 200; from M2 every 200-point enemy does: Blasmusiker, E-Biker, D-046). The chain resets on landing.

### Scoring, lives, timer, checkpoint

| Thing | Value |
|---|---|
| Oblate | 200 points, +1 Oblate (100 → +1 life) |
| Stomp | chain above |
| Brick broken | 50 |
| Power-up eaten | 1000 |
| Time bonus at the goal | 50 per remaining second (tallied 1 s per frame) |
| Maibaum (player's feet height h above the pole base at first touch) | h < 2 tiles: 100 · 2–4: 400 · 4–6: 800 · 6–7: 2000 · ≥ 7 tiles (Wurstkranz): **5000** + popup „Wurstkranz!" |

- **Lives**: start with 3, max 99. Score popups (small numbers) float up 16 px over 30 frames above whatever scored.
- **Timer**: `time_limit` seconds, counts down every 60 frames during Playing only. At ≤ 60 the ZEIT value turns red. At 0 the player dies; „Zeit is' um!" is shown during the death.
- **Checkpoint respawn**: after a death the whole level is reset to its initial state (enemies, boxes, bricks, Oblaten, like SMB); only the checkpoint flag, score, Oblaten counter and lives survive. The timer is reset to `time_limit`. The player respawns on the ground at the checkpoint (or the start) as Normal.
- **Game over** → „Weiter?": START restarts the current stage from its start (not the checkpoint) with 3 lives, score 0, Oblaten 0.

### Goal sequence

Touch the Maibaum → enemies freeze, timer stops, height score popup → Resi slides down the pole at 2 px/frame → at the base she hops off and walks right at walk speed past the Ortsende sign and off screen → time bonus tally → stage-clear text („Etappe geschafft!" + „Weiter nach St. Gilgen.") for 180 frames. In M1 (only one stage) this is followed by „Fortsetzung folgt …" for 180 frames, then the title screen. ### Climb camera (`[meta] camera = "climb"`, D-054)

Horizontal behaviour is unchanged (`follow_x = 144`, never scrolls left except D-051). Vertically it is SMW-style: it settles on the ground she stands on instead of following every jump. Today's dead-zone camera would bob up and down with every jump in a tall level, and after a climb it leaves only ~3 tiles visible above her head. Every `camera = "side"` level (all M1/M2 levels, the default) keeps today's dead-zone camera exactly.

| Rule | Value (tuning `[camera]`) |
|---|---|
| **Grounded** (on a tile, a one-way tile or a moving platform; also during the Durchkriechstein crawl) | target `camera.y = settle_feet − climb_feet_y` with `climb_feet_y = 148` (the framing of the street in Stages 1–3); the camera moves towards the target by at most `climb_settle_speed = 2` px per frame. The feet height of the last grounded frame is remembered as `ground_feet` |
| **Settle feet, slack, reach** (D-065, E20) | the settle feet follow the ground down at once; up only once the feet are more than `climb_up_slack = 48` px (3 tiles) above them, and then by the excess, so a box or plank ≤ 3 tiles high keeps the framing of the ground below and a 4-tile step settles 16 px. The slack holds only while she stays within `climb_up_reach = 48` px (horizontally) of where she stepped onto that ground; walking further, the settle feet catch up with her feet (a staircase never leaves the camera low for good). If a landing leaves her top above `climb_top`, the camera catches up `climb_catch_up_speed = 6` px per frame faster than the settle |
| **In the air, upwards** | if the hitbox top is above screen y `climb_top = 40`: `camera.y = top − 40` at once (high bounces stay under the HUD). While the slack holds (settle feet `lag` px below `ground_feet`) the line is `climb_top − lag`, but at least `climb_top_min = 11`: a Normal walk jump from a held 3-tile box (apex top at screen y 11) leaves the camera still (a big/Hackl one follows about 8 px and settles back in about 4 frames), so it doesn't bob on the box; a run jump follows only as far as keeping the top at 11 needs (D-065) |
| **In the air, downwards** | if the feet are below screen y 148: `camera.y = feet − 148`, but never more than `climb_fall_follow = 80` px below the settle target of `ground_feet` (`camera.y ≤ ground_feet − 148 + 80`) |
| **Death line** | when the player's hitbox top is below the screen bottom (`top > camera.y + 180`) she dies like falling out of the level (same death, same cause). With the numbers above: drops up to ~8 tiles survive (6 tiles lands at screen y 164, on screen); a cleft ≥ 10 tiles deep kills ~40 frames after the take-off, without a long fall to the level bottom |
| Clamp | to the level as always; at the level bottom the old "below the level" death still applies |
| Start / respawn | the camera starts at the settle target (no settling animation) |
| Goal sequence | x fixed (D-040); y settles on her feet with `climb_goal_feet_y = 164` at 2 px/frame, so the goal meadow ends framed exactly as in Stages 1–3 (pole base at screen y 164, crown in the D-029 HUD gap) |
| Activation | in climb levels an entity activates when, as before, it is within `activate_margin` horizontally **and** its spawn cell is within 64 px (4 tiles) of the screen vertically. Without this, pilgrims and chamois high up the mountain would wake up off screen and come down unseen |
| Parallax | background layers may set `factor_y` (sixteenths, 0..=32, default 0): the layer is drawn `(max camera y − camera.y) × factor_y / 16` px lower than today's bottom-aligned position, so it sits exactly where it does today when the camera is at the level bottom and sinks as Resi climbs. Layers only ever move down, so the opaque sky behind them never shows a gap |

## Stage 6: Wolfgangsee (Milestone 5 scope)

**Premise / joke line**: The Linienschiff was full in St. Gilgen. At the St. Wolfgang Markt landing, where Stage 5 ended, the old paddle steamer „Kaiser Franz Josef I." takes Resi on board. It has crossed the lake since 1873, has run on diesel since 1954 and nowadays carries at most 100 passengers to spare its old hull. It even outlived the Ischlerbahn, which owned it from 1898 until 1957. The steamer only goes part of the way. Bus 150 doesn't swim, so Resi does what has always worked on this lake: she rows across on a Traundl, and when that's full too, she swims. The swans defend a lake they only moved to in 1950. The Gelsen take blood without an appointment. A regatta claims right of way. Somewhere in the middle Resi crosses Austria's only state border that moves by itself. Then comes Strobl, the town everybody drives through, where she finally goes ashore.

Length **260 columns** (4160 px), **17 rows**, `camera = "autoscroll"` with **`autoscroll = [8, 208]`** (D-075). The camera follows Resi as usual on the quay, then scrolls on its own at 0.75 px/frame from the moment she reaches the steamer until the Strobl shore is in view (200 columns, 4267 frames ≈ 71 s). After that the side camera takes over again for the landing and the Maibaum, so the goal sequence is the usual one. ### Auto-scroll camera (`[meta] camera = "autoscroll"`, D-075)

| Rule | Value (tuning `[camera]`) |
|---|---|
| Zone | `[meta] autoscroll = [from, to]` in columns (required with this camera, a parse error otherwise; `0 ≤ from < to ≤ width − 20`). The zone is where the camera's **left edge** lies in `[from × 16, to × 16)` |
| Lead-in | while the camera's x < `from × 16`: the side camera, unchanged. As soon as following would put it at or past `from × 16`, it is set to exactly `from × 16` and the **scroll** starts |
| Scroll | every frame of play (not frozen, paused, dying or in the goal sequence) the scroll position grows by `autoscroll_speed = 192` subpx (**0.75 px/frame**); the camera's x is its whole-px floor. Independent of the player. Vertical: the side camera's dead zone, unchanged |
| Lead-out | once the camera's x reaches `to × 16`, the side camera takes over again (and never scrolls left) |
| Left edge pushes | in the scroll, after the camera and the platforms moved: if her hitbox's left edge is left of the camera's x, she is moved right by the difference with normal tile collision. If a **solid** tile stops that move, she dies (**squeezed**, `DeathCause::Squeezed`, the normal hit death). A platform carrying her left past the edge doesn't move the camera (D-051 is off in the scroll): she is pushed across its deck instead |
| Right clamp | in the scroll her hitbox's right edge stays ≤ camera x + 320 − `autoscroll_right_margin = 64`. Her own movement and any carry stop there like at a wall. So she always sees ≥ 4 tiles ahead, and things entering the screen are ≥ 64 px away |
| Start and respawn | framed as in side levels (centre at `follow_x`). If that framing lies in the zone, the scroll resumes after **`autoscroll_hold = 60` frames** of play, so a respawn on the lantern pontoon starts calmly. Leaving the lead-in into the zone never holds |
| Goal | the goal column must be ≥ `to + 20` (lint), so the Maibaum sequence always runs with the side camera (D-029/D-040 unchanged) |
| Activation and timing | unchanged (one screen ahead of the camera). In the scroll the camera doesn't depend on the player, so every entity in the zone activates at a fixed scroll position: Traundl phases and regatta starts can be tuned exactly (after a respawn the hold shifts the entities that wake at once by ≤ 60 frames; the level-designer checks the respawn route with a replay) |
| Death line | none (side level). Falling out of the level still kills, but the zone has a lake bed everywhere |
| Tuning validation | `0 < autoscroll_speed < swim.speed` (a swimmer can always gain on the screen), `autoscroll_hold` 0..=600, `autoscroll_right_margin` 0..=160; the Dampfer's speed (16 × 256 / `dampfer_frames_per_tile`) ≥ `autoscroll_speed` (a standing rider is never pushed) |

**Fallback** (D-075, engine's call, record it if taken): ship the stage with `camera = "side"`. The level is built left to right anyway and stays fair: boats and enemies then activate by the player's progress instead of the scroll position. The squeeze rule, the right clamp and the hold go unused.

### Swimming (D-076)

Swim water is a new tile kind (`TileKind::Swim`; tiles `see_wasser` for the surface row, `see_wasser_tief` below it, `steg_pfahl` = water with a pier post in it). The Salzach and `wolfgangsee_water` in Stages 1–5 stay lethal (D-050). Swim water looks different: it is clear, and the lake bed shows through, as it does in the real lake with its ~8 m visibility. The first screen teaches it with the sign „Baden heute / erlaubt" and a bathing notch with Oblaten under water.

| Rule | Value (tuning `[swim]`, names the engine's) |
|---|---|
| **Swimming** | her **chest point** (hitbox centre x, `chest_px = 8` px below her hitbox top, every form) lies in a Swim tile. Checked every frame before she moves |
| **Surface** | the top edge of a Swim tile whose cell above is not Swim. **At the surface** = swimming with the chest point less than `surface_band = 8` px below a surface. Strokes and buoyancy never lift the chest point above the surface: she stops with it exactly on the water line (vy 0). Only a jump-out leaves the water upwards |
| Floating | at the surface, with DOWN not held and vy ≥ 0, she **floats**: vy = 0, no gravity (treading water needs no tapping). **DOWN held** lets her sink from the surface (the dive) |
| Sinking | otherwise vy += `gravity = 16` (0.0625 px/frame²) up to `max_sink = 256` (1.0 px/frame): from rest she sinks 12 px in ~20 frames and reaches the lake bed (feet 50 px below their floating height) in ~60 |
| **Stroke** | a new JUMP press below the surface band: vy = −`stroke_speed = 448` (1.75 px/frame), a rise of ~24 px (1.5 tiles) over 28 frames. Every new press strokes (no cooldown); holding JUMP does nothing more. Emits `Stroke`, **not** `Jumped` |
| **Jump-out** | a new JUMP press **at the surface**: vy = −`player.jump_speed_walk` (4.09 px/frame, no run bonus), then the normal airborne rules (held JUMP uses `gravity_hold`). Emits `Jumped`. Held, Normal Resi's feet rise ~53 px above the water (big ~45 px), enough for a deck 1 tile up; a tap reaches only ~11 px (big ~3 px) |
| Horizontal | LEFT/RIGHT accelerate by `accel = 16` towards ±`speed = 320` (**1.25 px/frame**, RUN makes no difference); with no input she slows by `decel = 8` to 0. Facing follows the input at once. Faster than the scroll (0.75): a swimmer gains 0.5 px/frame on the edge |
| Entering | on her first swimming frame vy is clamped to [−`stroke_speed`, `max_sink`] (the splash brakes a fall), and the jump buffer and coyote frames are cleared (a press buffered in the air never becomes a stroke or a jump-out). Coyote time off a deck works as in the air |
| Ground under water | standing on the lake bed is `on_ground`: she walks at swim speed, JUMP strokes (no jump from the bed). Where a bed rises so that her chest leaves the water (a beach), she simply walks out. Head bumps under water work as in the air |
| Power states | the same numbers in every form; only the hitbox height differs (the chest point is always 8 px below the top). At the surface Normal Resi's feet are 14 px under water, big Resi's 22 px |
| Contact while swimming | **every** enemy contact hurts: no stomps, no Fiaker/bus/regatta roof bounces. A jump-out is airborne from its next frame, so it can end in a stomp („Delfinsprung") |
| Hackl | a RUN press while swimming throws nothing. An axe touching swim water is removed (as with water) |
| Items | Mozartkugel, Zaunerstollen and the Hackl item touching swim water are lost (as with water). Oblaten may be placed in swim cells (entity on a tile, D-049 extended to Swim tiles) |
| Enemies | walking kinds treat swim water like water. Only the M5 kinds use it: the Schwan floats on it, the Gelse never enters it, the Regattaboot ignores tiles |
| No breath meter | Resi can stay under as long as she likes (SMB has no meter either; a meter is backlog) |
| Lint | every swim-water column ends on a solid tile inside the level (no swimmer can sink out of it); a surface tile's cell above is never solid over a swim column in the scroll zone |
| Renderer | `swim` anim in each form while swimming (a stroke restarts it at frame 0); optional `blase` bubbles every 40 frames while her chest is below the surface band (renderer only, from the frame counter) |

**Fallback** (D-076): **surface-only swimming**. The chest point is held at the surface (no sinking, no strokes, DOWN does nothing), and JUMP is the jump-out. Then swans and regatta boats can't be dived under, so the level gives every swan a deck within its charge range and every regatta lane a Traundl. The rest stays.

**Routes and pace (M5 review).** In the scroll zone the screen sets the pace: the solver's optimum (frame 4540, 75.7 s) is the scroll's 71 s plus the lead-in and lead-out, and the right clamp stops anyone from getting ahead. - **Boats and decks** (the intended reading, and the premise: the steamer, then the Traundl): Resi is out of every swan's reach, Gelsen can be stomped from still decks, a regatta sail can be jumped from Traundl D, and the air Oblaten (the steamer arcs, the lines over A/B/C/D, the raft and pontoon) and the Zaunerstollen perch are easy to collect.
- **Swimming at the surface**: swans charge her, the paddle wheel hurts her, Gelsen and regatta boats hit her, and every air Oblate costs a jump-out.
- **Walking on the lake bed**: totally safe but poor. Only the 19 Oblaten placed under water, and Gelsen park over her until their hunt runs out.
The premise itself ends with "when that's full too, she swims", so swimming is part of the fantasy and not an exploit. - The shore path St. Gilgen – Brunnwinkl – Fürberg (Victor-von-Scheffel-Steig, partly paved), Brunnwinkl as the start of the Falkenstein hike; bus 150 serves St. Gilgen, not the north shore – [komoot](https://www.komoot.com/smarttour/38298959), [Jana Meerman](https://janameerman.com/falkenstein-hike/)
- Waggle dance: a straight waggle run then a half-circle return, alternating sides (figure-eight); the run's angle to vertical gives the direction relative to the sun, its duration the distance – [NC State Extension](https://content.ces.ncsu.edu/honey-bee-dance-language), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waggle_dance)
- Colour vision: von Frisch trained bees to sugar-water dishes on blue paper among grey cards (published 1914) – [Lernhelfer](https://www.lernhelfer.de/schuelerlexikon/biologie/artikel/karl-von-frisch)
- Common knowledge (no source needed): Wolfgangsee boat line („Linienschiff") from St. Gilgen, Platzkonzerte of the local brass bands („Humtata"), the B158 and the bus running along the south shore, Nordic walking and e-bikes around the lake.

## Sources (facts used in Stage 4)

- Falkenstein pilgrim path St. Gilgen → Fürberg → over the Falkenstein → Ried → St. Wolfgang, waymarked as trail no. 28 from the Fürberg car park, lowest point 538 m, highest 807 m, ~200 m of steep ascent, 24 signposted points of interest (behind „Weg 28 / steil. Sehr.") – [salzkammergut.at: Der Falkenstein-Pilgerweg](https://www.salzkammergut.at/en/oesterreich-tour/detail/430010480/der-falkenstein-pilgerweg.html)
- From Fürberg the Kreuzweg climbs a steep wooded gorge to the Falkensteinkirche, which is built above a „Durchkriechstein"; stones that pilgrims carried uphill as penance, cup-marked stones read as traces of the saint and the devil, the Quellkapelle, a rocky saddle with chapels and the Aberseeblick, a waterfall and the descent to Ried; Wolfgang threw his axe from the Falkenstein to choose the church site ~4 km away; in the Middle Ages St. Wolfgang ranked with Rome and Santiago, and most pilgrims came over the Falkenstein – [oberoesterreich.at: Der Weg der Wallfahrer](https://www.oberoesterreich.at/oesterreich-tour/detail/100275/der-weg-der-wallfahrer.html)
- Falkensteinkirchlein: a Wolfgang chapel documented 1350; the present church built 1626 around a cave in the Falkensteinwand (Johann Wilhelm Lueger, Pfleger of Hüttenstein); the crawl-through stone (Schliefstein) was passed in silence and without looking back, and was said to let even a large person through if free of sin; ~300 000 pilgrims in peak years of the 16th century; hermits lived by the church 1659–1811; the Brunnkapelle (1669) over Wolfgang's spring; the Hackelwurfkapelle where he threw the axe, with a figure of Wolfgang throwing it – [Austria-Forum: Falkensteinkirchlein](https://austria-forum.org/af/AustriaWiki/Falkensteinkirchlein)
- Customs still practised in part: carrying stones (Steinetragen), the echo call, crawling through the stone cave, washing the eyes, turning an old cult stone – [salzkammergut.at: Falkensteinkirche](https://www.salzkammergut.at/oesterreich-poi/detail/430010230/falkensteinkirche.html?type=2026)
- St. Wolfgang (born 924, Bishop of Regensburg) withdrew to the Abersee (the old name of the Wolfgangsee) in 976; he threw his axe from the Falkenstein, and it landed ~5 km away on a rock above the lake where the market St. Wolfgang grew (behind „Hacklwurf / Rekord: 5 km" and the Hackl power-up); first pilgrimages documented around 1291 (behind „Pilgerstau / seit 1291") – [SalzburgerLand: 1100 Jahre heiliger Wolfgang](https://www.salzburgerland.com/de/magazin/1-100-jahre-heiliger-wolfgang/)
- The Wolfgangsee boat line stops at Fürberg and Ried-Falkenstein (the Fürberg landing at the start) – [salzkammergut.at: Wolfgangsee-Schifffahrt](https://www.salzkammergut.at/oesterreich-poi/detail/400639/wolfgangsee-schifffahrt.html)
- Common knowledge (no source needed): chamois (Gämsen) in the Northern Limestone Alps and the „Gamswild"/„Wildwechsel" traffic-sign idiom; „Achtung Steinschlag" signs and rockfall nets on Austrian mountain paths; the Postbus 150 runs along the south shore (Stages 2–3 sources), so the north-shore pilgrim path has no bus.

## Sources (facts used in Stage 5)

- Wallfahrtskirche and Pacher altar: Michael Pacher's Gothic winged altar was completed in 1481 and brought to St. Wolfgang by ship and cart. It is 12.5 m high and over 6 m wide open, with the Coronation of the Virgin in the shrine, and is called one of the most beautiful winged altars (for the `wallfahrtskirche` prop and the church's place in the stage) – [salzkammergut.at: Michael-Pacher-Altar](https://wolfgangsee.salzkammergut.at/en/oesterreich-poi/detail/430010077/michael-pacher-altar.html?type=2026). The altar is of Swiss stone pine. Thomas Schwanthaler's baroque double altar stands in the north aisle. Around 1500 St. Wolfgang was among Europe's most important pilgrimage sites. The church was built where the saint's axe was found after he threw it (behind „Hier landete / das Hackl" and the `H`-box). A wolf, not a person, was the first to enter the church the devil helped build (legend; not used in the stage) – [Austria-Forum: Heimatlexikon Wolfgangsee](https://austria-forum.org/af/Heimatlexikon/Wolfgangsee); the church as one of Europe's most important pilgrimage places – [salzkammergut.at: Wallfahrtskirche St. Wolfgang](https://www.salzkammergut.at/en/oesterreich-poi/detail/430010229/pilgrimage-church-of-st-wolfgang.html)
- Pilgerbrunnen: the Gothic fountain in front of the church, built 1515 to supply the pilgrim crowds, who took the healing „Wolfgangi-Wasser" home (behind „Pilgerwasser / gratis") – [Austria-Forum: Heimatlexikon Wolfgangsee](https://austria-forum.org/af/Heimatlexikon/Wolfgangsee). It was built by Abbot Wolfgang Haberl in honour of the saint; it gave water to pilgrims and the neighbouring houses, and is a canopied fountain on the Markt – [salzkammergut.at: Pilgerbrunnen](https://www.salzkammergut.at/oesterreich-poi/detail/402140/pilgerbrunnen.html?type=2026)
- Hotel „Im Weissen Rössl": over 500 years of hosting guests, first pilgrims; the present inn opened in 1878; the Peter family since 1912, now in the fifth generation. Blumenthal and Kadelburg wrote the comedy in 1896, and Benatzky's operetta premiered in Berlin on 8 November 1930. The hotel calls its lake pool the world's first floating jacuzzi – [weissesroessl.at: History](https://www.weissesroessl.at/en/hotel/weisses-roessl/history.html). The operetta's characters include the hostess Josepha Vogelhuber, her head waiter (Zahlkellner) Leopold, Sigismund and Emperor Franz Joseph. Its songs include „Im weißen Rössl am Wolfgangsee" (Benatzky), „Was kann der Sigismund dafür" (Gilbert), „Mein Liebeslied muss ein Walzer sein", „Die ganze Welt ist himmelblau" (Stolz) and „Zuschau'n kann i net" (Granichstaedten) (behind the singing head waiter, „Zahlkellner / gesucht" and „Glück steht / vor der Tür") – [Wikipedia: The White Horse Inn (operetta)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_White_Horse_Inn_(operetta)), [SRF: the title song by Benatzky](https://www.srf.ch/radio-srf-musikwelle/schlagermosaik/peter-alexander-zoff-und-zensur-im-weissen-roessl). Common knowledge: the title song's refrain „da steht das Glück vor der Tür" (behind the tagline and the door sign). The hotel's restaurant really is called the „Kaiserterrasse" (Poll's Kaiserterrasse, Markt 74; behind the stage's Kaiserterrasse with the Zaunerstollen) – [salzkammergut.at: Poll's Kaiserterrasse](https://wolfgangsee.salzkammergut.at/en/oesterreich-gastronomie/detail/53600002/romantik-hotel-br-im-weissen-roessl.html)
- Schafbergbahn: since 1893 from St. Wolfgang to the Schafberg summit (1,783 m); the steepest steam cog railway in Austria; max. gradient 26 % (the stage's 1:4 track and „26 % steil"); 5.85 km, 1,190 m of ascent, about 35 minutes (behind „Zum Gipfel / 35 Minuten"); not electrified; historic steam locomotives among the oldest working ones in the world, plus modern steam locomotives; the valley station is on the Ried side of the village – [salzkammergut.at: SchafbergBahn](https://www.salzkammergut.at/oesterreich-poi/detail/400929/schafbergbahn.html). The modern oil-fired steam locomotives Z11 (1992) and Z12–Z14 (1995) were built by SLM Winterthur – [5schaetze.at: Typenblatt Z11–Z14](https://www.5schaetze.at/content/dam/tourism/downloads/typenblätter-/sbb/Typenblatt%20Z11-Z14.pdf), [Parlament: Anfragebeantwortung on the new steam locomotives](https://www.parlament.gv.at/dokument/XVIII/AB/3840/imfname_444135.pdf). Construction began 1892; the summit view takes in many lakes – [Austria-Forum: Heimatlexikon Wolfgangsee](https://austria-forum.org/af/Heimatlexikon/Wolfgangsee)
- Wolfgangseer Advent: Christmas markets in St. Gilgen, Strobl and St. Wolfgang linked by boat; its landmark is a 16 m floating Advent lantern on the lake (behind „Advent / ganzjährig" and the snow-globe stands) – [salzkammergut.at: Wolfgangseer Advent](https://www.salzkammergut.at/advent/oesterreich-veranstaltung/detail/430238269/wolfgangseer-advent.html?type=2026)
- Getting there: bus 150 (Salzburg – Bad Ischl) serves St. Gilgen and Strobl; for St. Wolfgang you change in Strobl to bus 546 (behind „Bus 150 / kommt nicht" → „Bus 546 / auch voll") – [wolfgangsee.salzkammergut.at: Location & arrival](https://wolfgangsee.salzkammergut.at/en/information/location-arrival.html)
- Bus park: Parkplatz 3 „Zentrum" is a large car and bus park at the eastern tunnel portal, three minutes' walk from the centre, with a bus day ticket (behind the bus park at the tunnel portal; the stage places it before the Markt) – [salzkammergut.at: Parkplatz 3 Zentrum](https://www.salzkammergut.at/oesterreich-poi/detail/103479/parkplatz-3-zentrum.html)
- Boats: steam navigation on the Wolfgangsee since 1873 (behind „Schifffahrt / seit 1873"); the boat line links St. Gilgen, Strobl and St. Wolfgang – [Austria-Forum: Heimatlexikon Wolfgangsee](https://austria-forum.org/af/Heimatlexikon/Wolfgangsee); the stops Ried-Falkenstein and Fürberg, and the Falkenstein pilgrim path ending at Ried: see the Stage 4 sources
- Common knowledge (no source needed): coach-tour day trips with short photo stops, snow globes and Christmas kitsch as souvenirs, pigeons on fountains, the lakeside promenade, staff shortages in tourism (behind „Zahlkellner / gesucht").

## Sources (facts used in Stage 6)

- Paddle steamer „Kaiser Franz Josef I.": ordered 1872 from the Linz shipyard, delivered in parts by road and assembled at Strobl, maiden voyage 20 May 1873 for the emperor's 25th jubilee; the first steamer on the Wolfgangsee; 33.1 m long; originally 228 passengers, **now limited to 100 to spare the old ship**; built with a two-cylinder steam engine driving two paddle wheels, **converted to a Deutz diesel in winter 1953/54** (Iveco diesel since 1999) (behind „Raddampfer / ohne Dampf", „Baujahr 1873 / läuft noch" and the Dampfer without steam puffs); **owned by the Salzkammergut-Lokalbahn (the Ischlerbahn) from 1898**, by the ÖBB after the railway closed in 1957, and since 2006 by Salzkammergutbahn GmbH (Salzburg AG, which also runs the Schafbergbahn); restored in 2008 to its 1908 look; carried the imperial family; cult status from „Im weißen Rössl" (1930) and later films – [Wikipedia (de): Kaiser Franz Josef I. (Schiff, 1873)](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaiser_Franz_Josef_I._(Schiff,_1873))
- Wolfgangsee Schifffahrt: in service since 1873 with the „Kaiser Franz Josef I." (33 m, still in the fleet next to motor ships, the flagship is the „Salzburg"); seven landings: St. Gilgen, Fürberg, Ried-Falkenstein, St. Wolfgang Schafbergbahn, St. Wolfgang Markt, **Gschwendt** and **Strobl** (behind the Gschwendt pier); St. Gilgen – Strobl takes 1 h 15 min – [salzkammergut.at: Wolfgangsee-Schifffahrt](https://www.salzkammergut.at/oesterreich-poi/detail/400639/wolfgangsee-schifffahrt.html)
- The lake's traditional boat: the flat-bottomed **Plätte**, also called **Traunerl** or **Fuhre** (the game's „Traundl"), with a long raised bow that serves as a landing ramp; pilgrims were rowed across to St. Wolfgang in it in the Middle Ages, and it carried animals, food and building material; it is usually rowed by one person, „like the gondolas in Venice" (behind „Wie Venedig / nur kälter"); an association keeps the tradition alive; a Plätte is shown at the Strobl Wassbad („Gschmå Platzl – Das Boot zum See") – [salzkammergut.at: Gschmå Platzl – Das Boot zum See](https://www.salzkammergut.at/oesterreich-poi/detail/430015143/gschmaa-platzl-das-boot-zum-see.html)
- The lake: ~10.3 km long, up to 2 km wide, max. depth ~113 m, average visibility 8.2 m (behind the clear swim water with the lake bed showing through); the state border between Salzburg and Upper Austria runs through the lake (the „Seidenfadengrenze", settled 1462/1689) and is **Austria's only self-shifting state border**, because the growing Ditlbach delta moves it, which a joint commission fixes since 1978 (behind „Landesgrenze / wandert"); **mute swans were brought to the lake in 1950, four birds, ~35 by 1961** (behind „Seit 1950 / Hausherr"); a ferry carries pedestrians and cyclists between St. Wolfgang and Abersee; the Strobl basin is shallower than the St. Gilgen basin; the lake drains at its east end into the Ischler Ache – [Austria-Forum: Wolfgangsee](https://austria-forum.org/af/AustriaWiki/Wolfgangsee)
- Regattas: the Union Yacht Club Wolfgangsee celebrated its 125th anniversary in 2026 with a regatta week of 71 classic wooden boats from six nations, called the largest traditional wooden-boat regatta in Austria so far, with the afternoon thermal wind (the „Brunnwind") arriving around midday (behind the Regatta section and „Regatta / Vorrang: wir") – [yacht.de: 71 Holzboote am Wolfgangsee](https://www.yacht.de/regatta/wettfahrten/71-holzboote-am-wolfgangsee-klassik-segler-feiern-125-jahre-union-yacht-club/)
- The floating Advent lantern: 16 m, the tallest structure on the lake, with ~0.5 km of LED strings; the Wolfgangseer Advent links its villages by boat (behind the `adventlaterne` on its pontoon; the stage's Advent lantern is unlit, it's summer) – [salzkammergut.at: Wolfgangseer Advent](https://www.salzkammergut.at/advent/oesterreich-veranstaltung/detail/430005373/wolfgangseer-advent.html)
- Water skiing exists on the lake (St. Gilgen's Wassersportzentrum offers water skiing and wakeboarding, May–September): the backlog's water skier is real – [salzkammergut.at: Wassersportzentrum Engel](https://www.salzkammergut.at/oesterreich-poi/detail/401222/wassersportzentrum-engel.html)
- Bus 150 runs along the south shore through Strobl (Stage 2 and 5 sources), so from the lake it is seen on the lakeside road (behind „Bus 150 / im Stau. Ha!"); Strobl is on line 150 and the next stage's „town everybody only drives through" (behind „Strobl: / bitte halten").
- Common knowledge (no source needed): „Gelsen" is the Austrian word for mosquitoes, and they gather in reed belts on summer evenings („Blutspende / ohne Termin"); mute swans defend their territory and hiss with arched wings (the Schwan's telegraph); „Baden erlaubt/verboten" signs at Austrian lakes; summer traffic jams on the lakeside road.
